ClamAV (Clam AntiVirus) is a [free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software), cross-platform [antimalware toolkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antivirus_software) able to detect many types of [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware), including [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_virus). It was developed for [Unix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix) and has third party versions available for [AIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_AIX), [BSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Software_Distribution), [HP-UX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP-UX), [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux), [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS), [OpenVMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenVMS), [OSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tru64_UNIX) (Tru64), [Solaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solaris_(operating_system)) and [Haiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiku_(operating_system)). As of version 0.97.5, ClamAV builds and runs on [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows).[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamAbout-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-Ports-4) Both ClamAV and its updates are made available free of charge. One of its main uses is on [mail servers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail_transfer_agent) as a server-side [email virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_virus) scanner.

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=1)]

ClamAV was initially released with version 0.10 on 8 May 2002, by Polish university student Tomasz Kojm.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-clamav20-5) In 2007, it was acquired by [Sourcefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourcefire),[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-techrepublic-6) which in turn was acquired by [Cisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco) in 2013[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-techcrunch-7) and now operates under its [Talos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco_Talos) cybersecurity division.

### Patent lawsuit

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=2)]

In 2008, [Barracuda Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracuda_Networks) was sued by [Trend Micro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trend_Micro) for its distribution of ClamAV as part of a security package.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-8) Trend Micro claimed that Barracuda's utilization of ClamAV infringes on a [software patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_patent) for filtering viruses on an [Internet gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_(computer_networking)). The [free software community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software_community) responded in part by calling for a [boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott) against Trend Micro. The boycott was also endorsed by the [Free Software Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Software_Foundation).[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-9) Barracuda Networks counter-sued with IBM-obtained patents in July 2008.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-10) On 19 May 2011, the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office issued a Final Rejection[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-11) (a determination by the patent examiner that the claims in a patent application are unpatentable, which is followed by the closure of the application) in the reexamination of Trend Micro's U.S. patent 5623600.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-12)

### Release history

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=3)]

In 2026, two security releases fixed bugs dating back to as old as 2004.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-13) Another 2026 announcement indicated seven open CVEs relating to bugs in file parsers and ZIP parsers that could lead to memory corruption.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-14)

## Features

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=4)]

ClamAV includes a command-line scanner, automatic database updater, and a scalable multi-threaded [daemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daemon_(computer_software)) running on an anti-virus engine from a shared library.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamAbout-3) The application features a [Milter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milter) interface for sent mail and on-demand scanning. It recognizes:

- [ZIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIP_(file_format)), [RAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAR_(file_format)), [Tar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tar_(computing)), [Gzip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gzip), [Bzip2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bzip2), [OLE2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OLE2), [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_(file_format)), [CHM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiled_HTML), [BinHex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BinHex), and [SIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIS_(file_format)) formats
- Most mail file formats
- [ELF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executable_and_Linkable_Format) and [Portable Executable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_Executable) (PE) files compressed with [UPX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPX), FSG, Petite, NsPack, wwpack32, MEW, and Upack, or obfuscated with SUE, Y0da Cryptor.
- [Office Open XML file formats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Open_XML_file_formats), [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML), [Rich Text Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Text_Format) (RTF) and [Portable Document Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_Document_Format) (PDF).[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamAbout-3)

The ClamAV virus database is updated at least every four hours and as of 10 February 2017 contained over 5,760,000 virus signatures[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-15) with the daily update Virus DB number at 23040.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-AboutClamAV-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamHome-17)

### Real-time file scanning

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=5)]

On Linux systems using kernel 3.8 or later, ClamAV offers a [real-time protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_time_protection) feature under the name On-Access Scanning. This feature uses the [fanotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanotify) kernel API to prevent processes from accessing files that ClamAV has identified as malicious.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-:2-19) Beginning in version 0.102.0, the on-access scanning functionality was separated from ClamAV's main clamd daemon into the clamonacc application.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-20)

The bulk of configuration options for On-Access Scanning are available in clamd.conf, with other options such as log output available in command-line options.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-:2-19)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-21) Users can run multiple instances of clamonacc simultaneously with different configurations, allowing for customized protection settings for various directories.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-:1-22) On-Access Scanning operates in notify-only mode by default which does not prevent access to any files.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-:1-22)

Clam Sentinel is an open-source third-party companion program for ClamAV on Windows that offers real-time protection functionality.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-23) This software was last updated in July 2014.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-24)

## Effectiveness

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=6)]

In the 2008 [AV-TEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AV-TEST) comparison of antivirus tools, ClamAV scored poorly in on-demand detection, avoiding false positives, and rootkit detection.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-25)

In a Shadowserver six-month test between June and December 2011, ClamAV detected over 75.45% of all viruses tested, putting it in fifth place behind AhnLab, Avira, BitDefender and Avast. AhnLab, the top antivirus, detected 80.28%.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-26)

In 2022 [Splunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splunk) conducted an efficacy study involving 416,561 malware samples sourced from [MalwareBazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MalwareBazaar?action=edit&redlink=1), bucketed as follows: 106135 Banking Trojans (trojans targeted towards stealing financial information); 26875 Botnets (malware for making the victim a part of a botnet); 190371 Information Stealers (programs designed to steal client information. E.g. [Keyloggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystroke_logging)); 52422 Loaders (program that loads one or more other malicious programs – that is, a stager that fetches harmful things directly into memory); 1321 Miners (crypto currency miners); 30251 RATs (Remote access tools. E.g. Backdoors); and 8273 [Trojans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_horse_(computing)) (a generic multipurpose malware that harms the user in different ways – generally disguises itself and delivered by tricking the user). Splunk's study concluded ClamAV was 59.94% effective overall at detecting commodity malware – being able to detect 249,696/416,561 samples.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-27)

In that same study, ClamAV performed relatively well at detecting certain types of malware in certain types of files (E.g. DOCX files, DIL files, ELF files, DOC files and EXE files), but was less effective in detecting malware in [JAR files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAR_(file_format)), [JS files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JavaScript), VBS files, Z files, [RAR files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAR_(file_format)), and XLSB files. In addition, ClamAV performed well in detecting a few top level categories of malware like Trojans & Botnets but performed poorly on other malware types like Crypto Miners, RATs and Info Stealers.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-28)

## Unofficial databases

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=7)]

The ClamAV engine can be reliably used to detect several kinds of malicious files. In particular, some [phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phishing) emails can be detected using antivirus techniques. However, false positive rates are inherently higher than those of traditional malware detection.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-29)

There are several unofficial databases for ClamAV:

- Sanesecurity is an organization that maintains a number of such databases; in addition, they distribute and classify a number of similar databases from other parties, such as Porcupine, Julian Field, MalwarePatrol.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-sanesecurity-30)
- SecuriteInfo.com also provides additional signatures for ClamAV.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-securiteinfo-31)

ClamAV Unofficial Signatures are mainly used by system administrators to filter email messages.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-32) Detections of these groups should be scored, rather than causing an outright block of the "infected" message.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-sanesecurity-30)

## Platforms

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=8)]

### Linux, BSD

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=9)]

ClamAV is available for [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux) and [BSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Software_Distribution)-based operating systems.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamAbout-3) In most cases it is available through the distribution's repositories for installation.

On Linux servers ClamAV can be run in daemon mode, servicing requests to scan files sent from other processes. These can include mail exchange programs, files on [Samba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_(software)) shares, or packets of data passing through a proxy server.

On Linux and BSD desktops ClamAV provides on-demand scanning of individual files, directories or the whole PC.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamAbout-3)

### macOS

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=10)]

[macOS Server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS_Server) has included ClamAV since version 10.4. It is used within the operating system's email service. A paid-for graphical user interface is available from Canimaan Software Ltd[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-:0-33) in the form of ClamXav.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-ClamXAV-34) Additionally, [Fink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fink_(software)), [Homebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homebrew_(package_management_software)) and [MacPorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacPorts) have ported ClamAV.

Another program which uses the ClamAV engine on macOS is Counteragent. Working alongside the [Eudora Internet Mail Server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eudora_Internet_Mail_Server) program, Counteragent scans emails for viruses using ClamAV and also optionally provides spam filtering through [SpamAssassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpamAssassin).

### OpenVMS

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=11)]

ClamAV for [OpenVMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenVMS) is available for [DEC Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEC_Alpha) and [Itanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itanium) platforms. The build process is simple and provides basic functionality, including library, the clamscan utility, the clamd [daemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daemon_(computing)), and freshclam for update.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-OpenVMS-35)

### Windows

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=12)]

There are [IA-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IA-32) and [x64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X64) variants of ClamAV available for Windows; additionally, [Cisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco_Systems)'s [Immunet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunet) uses ClamAV as its engine.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-36)

### OS/2

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=ClamAV&action=edit&section=13)]

A port of ClamAV is available for [OS/2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OS/2) (including [eComStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EComStation) and [ArcaOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcaOS)) with a native UI written in [REXX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REXX).[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-os2-ui-37)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClamAV#cite_note-38)
